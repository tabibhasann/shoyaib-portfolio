From the First Life
to You and Me

Science•24 November 2026

How tiny cells, too small to see, slowly changed into fish, dinosaurs, birds, mammals and people. Let’s travel through time again!

Did you know? If the whole history of Earth were squeezed into one day, the first life would appear at about 3 in the morning, oxygen would fill the air just before lunch, the first animals would arrive at about 9 at night, the dinosaurs would vanish at 11:39 pm, and humans would arrive only 6 seconds before midnight!

1 Tiny life rules the sea

From about 3.5 billion years ago

For a very, very long time, the only living things on Earth were tiny cells, far too small to see. They lived in the water and stuck together in slimy mats. Layer by layer, these mats built special rocks called stromatolites. For about 3 billion years, tiny life was the only life!

Inside a stromatolite. Each thin stripe was built by tiny living things, one layer at a time. (Picture: Didier Descouens, CC BY-SA 4.0)

Time moves on!

2 Oxygen fills the air

About 2.4 billion years ago

Some tiny cells called cyanobacteria learned a clever trick. They used sunlight to make their food, and they gave out a gas called oxygen. Bit by bit, oxygen filled the sea and then the air. Today we need oxygen to breathe, so we can thank these tiny cells!

Rusty stripes in rock. The new oxygen made iron in the sea rust, and the rust sank to make these red stripes. (Picture: Graeme Churchard, CC BY 2.0)

Time moves on!

3 Cells team up

About 2 billion years ago

One day, a big cell swallowed a smaller cell. But instead of eating it, the two lived together! The small cell became a tiny power station inside the big one. This made a new, bigger kind of cell. Plants, animals and you are all made of this kind of cell.

A cell like the ones in your body. The little bean-shaped parts, called mitochondria, were once tiny living things of their own! (Picture: LadyofHats, public domain)

Time moves on!

4 The first soft animals

About 600 to 540 million years ago

Earth may have frozen over almost completely, like a giant snowball. When the ice melted, something new appeared: the first animals made of many cells. They were soft and flat, a bit like jelly mats lying on the sea floor. They had no eyes, no legs and no shells.

Dickinsonia was one of the very first animals. It looked like a flat, stripy pancake! (Picture: Ghedoghedo, CC BY-SA 3.0)

Time moves on!

5 The animal explosion

About 540 million years ago

Then, in a short time, the sea filled with all kinds of new animals. Now they had eyes, legs, shells and teeth. Some were hunters and some were hiding. Scientists call this the Cambrian explosion. The first fish also swam in the sea at this time.

Anomalocaris was the top hunter of its time. It grabbed food with two spiky arms in front of its mouth. (Picture: Nobu Tamura, CC BY 3.0)

Trilobites had hard shells and good eyes. These fossils show them from small babies to grown-ups. (Picture: John Alan Elson, CC BY-SA 4.0)

Time moves on!

6 Life moves onto land

About 470 to 375 million years ago

For a long time, the land was bare rock. First, tiny plants crept out of the water. Then came bugs. Later, some fish with strong fins started to crawl in shallow water. Their great-great-great-grandchildren became frogs, lizards, and in the end, us!

Cooksonia was one of the first plants on land. It was only as tall as your finger! (Picture: Smith609, CC BY 3.0)

Tiktaalik was a fish with fins like little legs. It could prop itself up in shallow water. (Picture: Nobu Tamura, CC BY 2.5)

Time moves on!

7 The age of dinosaurs

About 230 to 66 million years ago

Dinosaurs ruled the Earth for more than 160 million years! Some were as big as a house and some were as small as a chicken. Many had feathers, and some of them became birds. Then, 66 million years ago, a giant space rock crashed into Earth, and most dinosaurs disappeared.

Tyrannosaurus rex, one of the biggest meat-eating dinosaurs ever. (Picture: Matt Martyniuk, CC BY-SA 3.0)

Archaeopteryx, half dinosaur and half bird. You can see its feathers in the stone! (Picture: H. Raab, CC BY-SA 3.0)

The space rock that ended the dinosaurs was about 10 km wide. It hit the sea near Mexico. (Picture: Donald E. Davis / NASA, public domain)

Time moves on!

8 Mammals take over

After 66 million years ago

Small furry animals called mammals had lived next to the dinosaurs, hiding and staying small. When the dinosaurs were gone, mammals grew bigger and bigger. They became whales, elephants, horses, cats, monkeys and apes.

Purgatorius was about as big as a mouse. It was one of the earliest relatives of monkeys, apes and people. (Picture: Nobu Tamura, CC BY 3.0)

The woolly mammoth lived in the cold Ice Age, and early people saw it alive! This is a model in a museum. (Picture: Flying Puffin, CC BY-SA 2.0)

And then...

9 Humans arrive!

About 300,000 years ago

Millions of years ago, some apes in Africa started to walk on two legs. Their family slowly changed, and about 300,000 years ago, people like us appeared. We learned to make tools, use fire, talk, paint and tell stories. Then we spread all over the world!

Lucy lived about 3.2 million years ago. She was a relative of humans who walked on two legs. (Picture: 120, CC BY 2.5)

The oldest Homo sapiens ever found! This skull, called Jebel Irhoud 1, was found in Morocco in 1961. In 2017, scientists showed it is about 300,000 years old. (Picture: Jonathan Chen, CC BY-SA 4.0)

Cave paintings made by people about 17,000 years ago. Early humans loved to draw animals! (Picture: Lascaux cave, public domain)

You are part of this story! Every living thing today, from a tiny ant to a giant whale, comes from those first tiny cells.

? Big mysteries still to solve

Scientists know a lot, but some questions are still open:

• Why did tiny life stay tiny for such a long time?
• What exactly started the animal explosion?
• Is there life like ours on other planets?

Maybe you will be the scientist who finds the answer one day!
